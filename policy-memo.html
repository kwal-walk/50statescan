About This Memo

This policy memo was prepared as part of a Master of Public Health capstone project. It analyzes Missouri House Bill 2355 — legislation that would direct the Missouri Department of Social Services to pursue a federal §1115 Medicaid demonstration waiver to cover Food Is Medicine interventions for eligible Medicaid beneficiaries.

The memo evaluates the bill's policy context, reviews supporting evidence, identifies implementation considerations, and offers recommendations for Missouri policymakers and stakeholders.

Policy Memo

TO: Missouri Policymakers and Medicaid Stakeholders FROM: Katherine Walcott, MPH Candidate, Health Management & Policy DATE: Spring 2026 RE: HB 2355 — Medicaid §1115 Waiver for Food Is Medicine Interventions in Missouri

Purpose

This memo provides an analysis of Missouri House Bill 2355 and offers policy recommendations regarding the feasibility and advisability of pursuing a federal §1115 Medicaid demonstration waiver to cover Food Is Medicine (FIM) interventions for eligible Missouri Medicaid beneficiaries.

Background

Food Is Medicine refers to the provision of healthy food — including medically tailored meals, produce prescriptions, and medically tailored groceries — as part of a patient's clinical care plan, integrated with and paid for by the health care sector. Nationally, at least 20 states have received or are pursuing §1115 waivers that include nutrition-related services.

Missouri HB 2355 would direct the Department of Social Services to apply for a §1115 waiver to enable the state Medicaid program to cover FIM interventions for beneficiaries with diet-sensitive chronic conditions. Missouri has not yet approved a §1115 waiver covering nutrition services.

Policy Analysis

A §1115 waiver would allow Missouri to test a novel benefit structure and collect state-specific outcome data — a critical step toward establishing a permanent Medicaid benefit. Key considerations include federal budget neutrality requirements, the need for an evaluation plan, the feasibility of identifying and contracting with FIM providers, and population targeting.

Evidence from other states and peer-reviewed research suggests that FIM interventions — particularly medically tailored meals — can reduce hospitalizations, lower total cost of care, and improve self-reported health status among high-need Medicaid beneficiaries. Cost-effectiveness analyses have found ICERs well below standard U.S. willingness-to-pay thresholds. (See the Evidence tab for a full review of the economic literature.)

Recommendations

  1. Support passage of HB 2355 and direct DHSS to begin §1115 waiver application development, prioritizing populations with the highest burden of diet-sensitive conditions.
  2. Engage managed care organizations (MCOs) early in the waiver design process to ensure alignment with Medicaid managed care contracts and leverage existing ILOS authority where feasible.
  3. Build in robust evaluation requirements from the outset, including data collection on health outcomes, cost of care, and food security — to support renewal and eventual state plan amendment.
  4. Engage community-based food providers and health systems to assess local FIM delivery capacity and identify gaps prior to waiver approval.
  5. Consider a phased approach — beginning with medically tailored meals for the highest-acuity Medicaid beneficiaries, where evidence is strongest — to manage implementation risk and build the state-specific evidence base.

Conclusion

HB 2355 represents a timely and evidence-informed policy opportunity for Missouri. A well-designed §1115 waiver would allow the state to join a growing national movement integrating food-based interventions into Medicaid, with the potential to improve health outcomes and reduce costs for some of the most vulnerable Missourians. Careful attention to waiver design, provider capacity, and evaluation will be essential to long-term success.
